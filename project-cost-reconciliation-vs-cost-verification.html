Project Cost Reconciliation vs Project Cost Verification on Alliance Contracts

Why explaining cost growth needs more than confirming actual costs

Tech Aid Consultants · October 2026 · 3 min read

← Back to services

Alliance and collaborative contracts share risk between the owner and contractor through pain/gain arrangements. For that to work, the alliance needs to know not only what has been spent, but why cost has moved from the tender. Project cost verification answers the first question. Project cost reconciliation answers the second.

What project cost verification does

Cost verification, usually carried out by an auditor or verifier, confirms that claimed costs are genuine, correctly coded and reimbursable. It gives the owner financial assurance that money spent was spent on the project.

What it does not show

A verified cost shows that money was spent, not why more or less was spent than the tender assumed.

Design development versus scope change

Some design growth is normal development within the original scope; some is genuine scope change. Both appear as verified cost and are hard to tell apart without reference to the tender scope.

Tender assumptions versus delivery conditions

Tender estimates rely on assumptions such as production rates and supplier quotes. When delivery differs, the cost is real and verifiable, but the ledger does not show which assumption moved.

Without this separation, the alliance sees a single figure for cost growth, and pain/gain and risk-share discussions have little to work with.

What project cost reconciliation adds

Cost reconciliation keeps the tender estimate as a live baseline through delivery and separates cost movement by source:

  • Design development – growth within the original scope
  • Scope change – client-directed and third-party variations
  • Delivery conditions – production rates, supplier rates and rise and fall against tender assumptions

What reconciliation relies on

Reconciliation relies on the contractor to identify design changes and variations, and to provide delivery data such as achieved production rates and purchase order prices.

TAC separates and reports the cost of each movement using the information the contractor provides. We do not audit that information or decide what is a valid variation – those remain matters for the alliance team.

When to start

Reconciliation is most effective from the start of delivery, with the tender estimate set up as a structured, linked baseline. Starting later is possible but less precise.

In summary

  • Project cost reconciliation reports cost movement by source against the tender baseline.
  • Project cost verification confirms that actual costs are valid.
  • Together, they give the alliance a clearer basis for pain/gain and risk-share discussions.
